# M15 — Composition Director 2.0: Motifs, Roles and Transitions

**Status:** Implementation landed on `main`; 66 regression tests pass on Linux Python 3.10–3.12 and Windows Python 3.11. Subjective listening validation remains pending.  
**Purpose:** Turn the existing formal-section and phrase-function systems into a coherent composition layer with persistent musical memory.

## 1. Persistent motif identity

The motif planner extracts a source phrase from a stable, independent motif-anchor stream. It stores a stable motif ID, source section, phrase length, pitch centre and a relative sixteenth-note sequence containing pitch, duration, velocity and pan. The identity remains stable across all section instances.

The melodic render repeats the transformed motif within each section. The motif anchor is independent from the melody-regeneration stream: regenerating the melody can change its expression and synthesis while leaving the hat/snare shadow derived from the anchor unchanged.

## 2. Section transformation grammar

Each section receives one explicit deterministic operation:

| Operation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `repeat` | Preserve the source phrase without changing its contour |
| `return` | Recall the anchor phrase |
| `displacement` | Move the phrase by a sixteenth-note cell |
| `fragmentation` | Retain selected events and leave deliberate gaps |
| `augmentation` / `diminution` | Expand or compress the rhythmic phrase |
| `inversion` | Reflect pitches around the motif pitch centre |
| `retrograde` | Reverse the phrase order |
| `rhythmic_shadow` | Move the phrase to a neighbouring rhythmic layer |
| `call_response` | Add a mirrored response in the latter half of the phrase |
| `dissolve` | Reduce the phrase to a quieter residual fragment |

Only one principal transformation is chosen per section. The source motif ID persists and each instance records the operation and its section bounds.

## 3. Shared rhythmic motif across roles

Melody is the main motif carrier. During development/uplift/contrast the transformed rhythm can also place quiet hi-hat shadows. In call/response sections it can place restrained snare responses. These accents use a separate role relationship and the same motif ID; they do not consume the kick/snare/hat pattern random streams. Their velocity is deliberately lower than the principal role pattern.

## 4. Seven-role orchestration plan

Every section receives a role intent for:
- kick;
- snare;
- hi-hat;
- break;
- melody;
- texture;
- ambience.

Each intent exposes activity, density, syncopation, register, brightness, space and a motif relationship such as anchor, response, shadow, carrier, counter-motion or handoff. Section functions have explicit orchestration profiles; they are not inferred from energy alone. Density deterministically thins non-anchor percussion hits, while syncopation can move eligible even-grid upper-role accents one sixteenth forward without crossing a bar. Metric anchors and motif-carried shadows/responses are preserved. Return sections preserve the anchor kick/motif register while development and contrast can redistribute activity among the independent roles.

## 5. Transition gestures

Outgoing phrase directives become explicit transition records with source/target section IDs, bar boundary, bounded start/end seconds, gesture and affected roles. Transition windows never extend beyond the source section. The role envelopes shape:
- pickup/fill accents;
- subtraction and rhythmic collapse;
- hard-cut/silence-gate fades;
- riser/reverse-swell support in texture and ambience;
- motif fragment/reassembly;
- register-shift and echo-handoff intentions.

The actual section boundary remains fixed; transition shaping does not lengthen or truncate the composition.

## 6. Independent composition axes

The Generator exposes separate controls for arrangement density, rhythmic syncopation, instability, melodic activity, melody register, tonal brightness and spatial intent. They are not aliases for the global composition-energy or structural-variation controls.

The project metadata stores these axis values, motif plan, section role plans and transition gestures for reproducibility and inspection.

## 7. Determinism and regeneration contract

The motif anchor uses a dedicated named stream. Cross-role accents are derived from the anchor, not from the mutable melody pattern stream. Melody regeneration can alter its own expression and synthesis without changing the drum/texture/ambience stems. Section role plans and transition windows are deterministic from the composition plan, phrase directives, axis settings and seed.

## 8. Validation and listening

Automated fixtures cover:
- motif extraction and stable identity;
- transformation semantics and recognisable return;
- repeated motif event timing and section-boundary containment;
- deterministic role plans and independent axis effects;
- transition alignment and continuous role envelopes;
- shared motif ID across melody and percussion accents;
- full-engine determinism and stem-isolated regeneration.

Listening remains necessary to judge contrast, continuity, register, negative space and whether a transition feels intentional. The automated suite cannot assert musical quality.

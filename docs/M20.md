# M20 — Perceptual orchestration and multi-scale phrase grammar

**Status:** Implemented — deterministic director integration and Score persistence; subjective listening sign-off remains experimentally unverified  
**Depends on:** M15 Composition Director, M17 Score-first pipeline; benefits from M18/M19.

## Goal

Make the director compose relationships between materials across event, cycle, phrase, section and whole-track time scales, while managing attention and negative space.

## Philosophy

The guides identify auditory grouping, masking, figure/ground, expectation, asymmetry, nested time and the value of subtraction. A dense track needs a hierarchy of attention, not simply more independent layers.

## Scope

1. **Hierarchical time model**
   - Event gestures and articulations.
   - Motif/variation cycles with independent lengths and phase.
   - Asymmetric phrase spans with explicit functions and optional overlap.
   - Sections and whole-track narrative/ending strategy.
   - Keep pulse, meter, pattern cycle and phrase boundaries independent.

2. **Role-pair grammar**
   - Unison/reinforcement/doubling.
   - Complement/interlock.
   - Call-response/antiphonal exchange.
   - Independent counterpoint and controlled convergence.
   - Relationship can change over the track, with explicit transition intent.

3. **Attention hierarchy**
   - Foreground, midground, background and intentional absence/suspension.
   - Planned hand-off between roles.
   - Optional register/spectral occupancy and onset-overlap warnings.
   - Never treat simple occupancy or masking heuristics as a quality score.

4. **Identity and mutation permissions**
   - Stable anchors and explicit dimensions permitted to change.
   - One principal development per phrase by default, with deliberate ruptures available.
   - Pattern, motif and sound identity can be preserved independently.

5. **Transitions and pacing**
   - Negative space, subtraction, silence, collapse, pickups, fills, return, residue and open/closed endings.
   - Transition should be audible at local and larger structural scales.

## Explicitly out of scope

General notation/arrangement editor, live scheduler and one universal form template.

## Acceptance criteria

- Plan is deterministic and fully inspectable in Score metadata.
- Phrase lengths may be asymmetric and not forced into 4/8-bar blocks.
- Cycle phases and planned alignments are tested through rational-beat time.
- Pairwise role grammars have deterministic rules and unit tests.
- Attention hand-offs and reserved silence are represented in generated intent.
- Existing M15 motif/transition fixtures remain valid or are versioned intentionally.

## Listening protocol

Audition the full mix, each role, and key role pairs. Identify the foreground before and after each transition. Check that the anchor is learned before a mutation, that independent cycles create meaningful alignment/drift, that layers have space to emerge, and that subtraction makes later returns more effective.

## Deliverables

- Hierarchical phrase/role relationship model.
- Attention and spectral/register intent representation.
- Asymmetric phrase and independent cycle planning.
- Listening fixture set for same-seed comparisons.

## Implementation log — M20.1–M20.3 foundation

- Added a versioned Score-native orchestration plan with rational-beat asymmetric phrase spans, independently phased cycles, role relationships, attention spans, explicit absence and mutation permissions.
- Pair grammars cover unison/reinforcement/doubling, offset complement/interlock/counterpoint, call-response/antiphonal exchange and time-bounded convergence.
- Attention includes foreground/midground/background/absence, hand-off identifiers and reserved silence. Register/spectral conflicts are heuristic warnings only, not quality scores.
- Score JSON and Reawarp MIDI metadata preserve orchestration intent; existing scores without the optional field remain compatible. This does not change playback scheduling or audio rendering.

### Remaining M20 work


## M20 close-out — director integration

The M20 plan is now compiled directly from the existing M15 generation decisions in `build_score_generation_plan`. This keeps one source of truth for section, phrase, motif, role and transition decisions.

- Each generated Score receives `orchestration_plan` and a duplicate human-inspectable `metadata.perceptual_orchestration` record. Score JSON and Reawarp MIDI retain the versioned plan.
- M15 section boundaries become rational-beat phrase spans using the active tempo. The final phrase ends at the exact requested composition duration rather than a rounded bar edge.
- Role cycle lengths are taken from generator semantics (bar pulse, backbeat, the configured break cycle, selected seven-pulse pattern family, and persistent motif phrase). Phase offsets are deterministic and domain-separated by role.
- Pattern relationships and M15 motif relations become time-bounded role-pair grammar spans. Their transition intent is retained.
- Every section receives a categorical attention assignment for all seven roles. Inactive roles are explicit absence/reserved silence; foreground changes carry shared hand-off IDs on the outgoing and incoming spans.
- Mutation policies inherit motif-preserved and motif-changed dimensions while keeping source identity stable by default and permitting at most one principal development per phrase.
- Existing transition gestures are represented on the same beat clock. Register/spectral occupancy and near-simultaneous multi-role events emit heuristic review warnings only, never a quality score.
- Added exact cycle-alignment queries, schema-v1 read compatibility, director integration tests, and a deterministic listening scenario set under `tests/fixtures/m20/listening_scenarios.json`.

### Listening sign-off

The authored scenarios cover anchor/return, cycle drift, foreground hand-off, reserved negative space and same-seed A/B. They are designed to be rendered and compared at matched level using the full mix, role solos and role pairs. Automated tests verify reproducibility and inspectable intent; they do not claim to replace human listening. Target-machine perceptual evaluation therefore remains experimentally unverified.

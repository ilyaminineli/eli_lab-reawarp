# M19 — Harmonic context and voice-leading

**Status:** M19.1–M19.3 implemented on main. M19.3 adds boundary-driven sequence planning, whole-path melodic voice assignment, explicit movement preferences, role/register targets, serializable constraints and stable deterministic tie-breaking.  
**Depends on:** M17 canonical Score; M18 control bus remains optional.

## Goal

Give the composition system optional harmonic context, sonority relationships, bass-direction planning and configurable voice-leading while preserving non-functional, modal, intervallic, spectral, microtonal and deliberately ambiguous music.

## Design principles

1. Harmonic context is an optional constraint/weight field, never mandatory chord progression.
2. A pitch collection is vocabulary, not an assumed tonal centre. Centre, stability, emphasis and harmonic rhythm are separate controls.
3. Hard constraints and soft preferences are explicit and inspectable. No single opaque beauty score.
4. Bass is an independent compositional role that may reinforce, interlock with, or oppose the kick.
5. All pitch-to-frequency conversion uses Score tuning reference data. MIDI 1.0 loss is documented rather than hidden.
6. Existing scores with no harmonic context retain their current chromatic behaviour.

## M19.1 — Pitch field and sonority primitives

### Pitch field
- Optional context ID and mode: chromatic, collection, centred, intervallic, spectral, microtonal, ambiguous.
- Optional centre/reference pitch in fractional pitch coordinates.
- Pitch collection and interval vocabulary.
- Per-pitch and per-interval stability/tension weights.
- Register bounds, preferred registers and role-specific ranges.
- Harmonic rhythm/sonority change positions in rational beats.
- Explicit absence of a centre or context is valid.

### Sonority
- Stable ID and onset/duration in rational beats.
- Pitch members in tuning-aware pitch coordinates, not pre-rounded MIDI notes.
- Optional bass/root reference, register/spread, tension metadata, and source/transformation provenance.
- Relations: retain, rotate, transpose, invert intervals, expand/contract, registral displacement, spectral derive, or replace.

### Score persistence
- Extend Score with an optional serializable harmonic-context/sonority section using a backward-compatible default.
- Validate unique IDs, rational timing, finite weights, legal bounds, and tuning references.
- Preserve absent context exactly through load/save round-trip.

## M19.2 — Sonority generation and transformation

**Status:** Implemented on main.

### Candidate generation
- Generate candidates across an explicit tuning-coordinate register and pitch step.
- Apply hard pitch-field constraints only when requested. Soft vocabulary/interval matches, stability and tension remain separately inspectable and only bias selection.
- Build ordered multi-voice sonorities with explicit voice count, register, minimum/maximum spacing, seed and temperature.
- Use a bounded deterministic beam search; stable tie-breaking and seeded temperature selection make repeated generation reproducible.
- Store generation seed, pitch-field mode, tuning identity, register and spacing settings as sonority provenance.

### Transformations
- Rotation raises a selected number of low voices by one tuning period.
- Transposition moves all voices and the optional bass reference by tuning steps.
- Inversion reflects pitches around an explicit axis.
- Expansion/contraction scales intervals around an explicit or derived pivot.
- Register displacement moves the sonority by integer tuning periods.
- Spectral derivation maps positive partial ratios into pitch coordinates using the tuning's period ratio and divisions.
- Voice replacement mutates one ordered voice while recording the previous and replacement pitch.

Every transformation creates a new sonority, preserves onset/duration and tension, and records its immediate source sonority ID, operation and parameters. No transformation edits the source object in place. Frequency resolution remains tuning-aware and does not quantise stored pitch coordinates.


## M19.3 — Sonority and voice-leading planner

**Status:** Implemented on main.

### Boundary model and sequence search

- Each `SonorityBoundary` defines a sonority change with rational-beat onset/duration, section ID, phrase ID, voice count and optional per-voice role targets.
- Boundaries may provide explicit pitch members or ask M19.2 to generate a sonority in the selected tuning/pitch field. Generated boundary seeds are derived with BLAKE2b from the plan seed and sonority ID; Python's process-randomised `hash()` is never used.
- `plan_sonority_sequence()` keeps melodic voice identities across the entire ordered sequence. A bounded beam search compares accumulated path costs instead of making isolated greedy pairwise decisions.
- The result exposes each voice's pitch path, adjacent assignments, crossed voice pairs, motion classification and per-transition cost breakdown. Section/phrase changes remain visible in every transition record.

### User-visible movement controls

- Cost weights are separate for motion distance, large leaps, register excursions, target-role affinity, common-tone retention, voice crossing, contrary motion, oblique motion, parallel motion and deliberate crossing.
- `VoiceRoleTarget` names each voice's role and can declare a preferred pitch and/or preferred register. Register limits can act as soft costs or hard constraints.
- Motion styles are independently selectable: `contrary`, `oblique` and `parallel`. Common-tone retention has its own enable switch and bonus. Crossing can be set to `avoid`, `neutral` or `prefer`.
- Hard constraints include maximum leap, minimum/maximum total movement, whether voices may cross, a maximum crossing count, a minimum number of common tones and hard role-register bounds.
- `VoiceLeadingPlanConfig`, boundaries, transitions and complete plans all round-trip through explicit dictionaries. `SonoritySequencePlan.attach_to_score()` stores sonorities and the serializable plan in Score; `from_score()` restores it.
- Equal-cost candidates are ordered by accumulated pitch-path tuples. Candidate pitch permutations and cost components have explicit stable ordering; no hash-table iteration order influences selection.

There is deliberately no cadence, tonic-resolution or tension-release constraint. A high motion/leap weight, minimum-motion constraint, crossing preference or contrary-motion preference can produce high-friction, unresolved trajectories. Constraints are only applied when explicitly configured.

### Implementation and sign-off

- Automated tests cover reproducibility, generated and explicit boundary sonorities, section/phrase transitions, all movement preferences, hard constraints, role registers, high-friction motion and Score round-trips.
- Listening remains necessary to assess whether a chosen motion style serves the phrase; the exposed costs explain the planner's decision but are not a universal musical-quality score.

## M19.4 — Bass role planner

Independent bass modes:
- pedal/drone;
- kick-locked reinforcement;
- syncopated ostinato;
- independent cycle;
- melodic bass;
- punctuation/answer gestures.

Bass plan uses shared phrase/form boundaries but owns its rhythm and pitch movement. Density, register, movement range, kick relationship and cadence avoidance are explicit controls.

## M19.5 — Motif and MIDI integration

- Motif transformations declare which identity dimensions are retained: contour, interval profile, rhythm, register, articulation and timbre.
- Sonority changes can transform a motif without erasing its identity.
- Resolve all pitch values through the Score tuning reference.
- MIDI 1.0 exports rounded integer semitone note numbers with exact Reawarp pitch/frequency metadata retained in text events. External MIDI players cannot reproduce arbitrary microtonal tuning without pitch-bend/MPE conventions.

## Acceptance criteria

- Existing chromatic projects render unchanged without harmonic context.
- Context and sonority records serialize/round-trip deterministically.
- Frequency calculations are mathematically correct for equal divisions and explicit period ratios.
- Pitch-field hard constraints are never violated; soft costs remain separately inspectable.
- Voice-leading sequence plans are deterministic, honor configured crossing/leap/register constraints, expose per-transition cost breakdowns, and serialize their weights, preferences and hard limits.
- The sequence planner can intentionally retain unresolved motion and high friction; it does not impose functional cadence rules.
- Bass patterns can move independently of percussion while retaining common phrase/form timing.
- Tests cover centred, modal, intervallic, spectral, microtonal and ambiguous fields plus MIDI precision boundaries.

## Listening protocol

Use one motif/rhythm with: unconstrained chromatic pitch, pedal-centred pitch, slowly changing sonorities, intervallic field, spectral field and non-12TET tuning. Level-match and assess continuity, tension, melodic identity, bass/percussion relationship, and whether harmonic direction emerges without over-explaining the material.

## Out of scope

Mandatory functional chord progressions, automatic tonal correction, opaque AI scoring, a DAW piano roll, and compulsory harmony on every score.

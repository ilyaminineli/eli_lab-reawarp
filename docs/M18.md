# M18 — Musical automation and control performance

**Status:** Partially implemented on `main`; the typed model, deterministic scoped sources, ScoreRenderer integration, per-note envelope/vibrato controls across five melodic palettes, and Score-aware scope suggestions are in place. Cross-platform CI passes 129 tests on Linux Python 3.10–3.12 and Windows Python 3.11. Planner authoring, remaining synthesis/FX destinations and critical listening remain open.  
**Depends on:** M16/M17 Score and typed AutomationControlBus  
**Cross-cutting gate:** M14 target-machine profiling and Pyo-enabled listening remain separate.

## Goal

Make score automation a compositional instrument. Musical movement should be writable and inspectable as control trajectories, not hidden inside renderer callbacks or represented only by post-FX wet curves.

## Philosophy

The IDM guides treat filter sweeps, pitch/register movement, decay, grain density, spatial movement, feedback and pattern probability as compositional material. Curves should create relationships between recurring events and larger phrase/section development. More automation is not automatically better: modulation depth and hierarchy must support the foreground and preserve recognisable sound identity.

## Implemented foundation

1. **Typed target catalogue**
   - Targets declare domain, unit, value type, legal range, interpolation, smoothing, hysteresis and update clock.
   - The default registry covers synthesis, event/role composition, sample contribution, spatial and fixed FX controls; MIDI CC targets are resolved through the typed MIDI pattern.
   - Target validation rejects incompatible units, unsupported interpolation and out-of-range values. The M13 stem-aware FX registry remains compatible with its established target IDs.

2. **Automation lane scopes**
   - Lanes support `global`, `section`, `role` and `event` scope.
   - Precedence is global → section → role → event. Absolute lanes replace the inherited value; relative lanes add an offset.
   - Smoothing and hysteresis settings are serialised with the lane. Older Score JSON defaults to a global, absolute lane with no smoothing or hysteresis.

3. **Deterministic control sources**
   - Reusable sources support `lfo`, `envelope`, `stepped`, `random_walk` and `phrase_curve` forms.
   - Beat-addressed source definitions preserve rational start/end positions. Random walks use a source-specific stable seed; all generators are bounded and reproducible.
   - Generated curves are materialised once per control-bus instance rather than regenerated on every sample lookup.

4. **Modulation relationships**
   - A relationship declares source, destination, amount, polarity, add/multiply mode, output range, optional base value, scope, smoothing, hysteresis and free/event/role/section retrigger.
   - Retrigger origins are resolved to the shared absolute frame clock; source-local start positions are retained when a source is phase-reset.
   - A modulation-only destination can specify an explicit base value, preventing a source from modulating an unintended zero baseline; an active lane takes precedence over that base.

5. **Score and render integration**
   - Score JSON round-trips lane scopes, sources and modulation relationships without changing the Score schema version; old M17 records remain readable.
   - ScoreRenderer builds event, role and section context from the canonical Score and evaluates controls against RenderTimeline, including tempo ramps.
   - Production melody voices consume synth cutoff/resonance/gain curves, pitch-at-event-onset, per-note attack/decay/release envelope times and bounded vibrato depth/rate that drives continuous pitch motion across subtractive, FM, additive, modal and granular palettes. Envelope and vibrato destinations declare event update clocks and resolve once per note; the oscillator then applies the vibrato trajectory continuously.

   - Event probability, velocity scale, role activity, sample contribution, mono pan and stereo width are also consumed by the event renderer and sample adapters.
   - Control-rate samples carry an active-state mask. A scoped lane/modulation that does not match an event—or whose source is outside its declared beat window—passes through the instrument's original parameter rather than applying an inactive default to unrelated roles.
   - Probability gates are stable for a score event and its generated sample/break trigger. Probability changes do not delete the symbolic event from the Score.
   - Sample preparation preserves M18 data and avoids duplicating generated trigger events when an existing project Score is re-rendered.
   - Existing M13 FX wet automation JSON remains a separate, compatible adapter into the same typed control bus.

6. **User workflow**
   - Stem Mixer now has separate **FX AUTOMATION** and **SCORE CONTROL** tabs.
   - The Score Control editor supports the currently rendered destinations, five source shapes, scope, phase/retrigger, amount and seed, with a curve preview and an Apply & Re-render action.
   - Role, section and event scopes suggest exact IDs from the loaded Score; the selector remains editable for custom IDs and caps large event lists for responsiveness.
   - Applying controls re-renders event-backed Score voices while preserving the project's specialist whole-field texture and ambience stems.
   - The editor is intentionally a first control-authoring surface, not yet a full multi-point curve drawing canvas or a modulation-matrix graph.

## Remaining work

1. **Planner authoring:** let composition planners consume role density, syncopation, activity and articulation controls at phrase/section boundaries. Their targets are registered, but planner-level regeneration from those curves is not yet implemented.
2. **Further synthesis expression:** connect wavetable position, partial balance and granular position/size/density only to instrument families that have a clear musical interpretation. The current continuous pitch motion is intentionally limited to a low-depth vibrato control rather than exposing unrestricted audio-rate pitch patching.
3. **FX/spatial breadth:** map the selected score-control destinations to the existing fixed FX routing pass where the project and signal-path semantics are unambiguous. The M13 JSON editor remains the supported FX wet automation workflow today.
4. **Authoring quality:** add editable multi-point curves, source parameter editing, destination-specific base-value presentation, template presets, and undo/reset. Score-aware scope suggestions are now present, but remain a compact selector rather than a full event browser.
5. **Validation and listening:** The current cross-platform CI matrix passed 129 tests per job on Linux Python 3.10–3.12 and Windows Python 3.11, including five synthesis-palette expression cases and the Score scope-selector regression. Retain a deterministic fixture pack and perform matched-level audition of no automation, one slow macro, an event-relative envelope and a coupled modulation. Listen for masking, transient loss, filter dulling, unstable pitch and movement that competes with the foreground.
6. **Performance:** profile control sampling at realistic event counts and sample rates; choose rates from destination update-clock requirements and use interpolation/caching without forcing audio-rate Python loops.

## Explicitly out of scope

Real-time MIDI/OSC control, a general-purpose modular modulation graph, arbitrary audio-rate patch cables, live audio followers, and replacement of deterministic offline rendering.

## Acceptance criteria

- Registered targets validate unit, range, value type and legal interpolation.
- Global/section/role/event scope and absolute/relative precedence are deterministic and serializable.
- Source definitions and modulation relationships round-trip through Score JSON; old M17 Score JSON remains readable.
- Fixed seed/config yields repeatable control trajectories and audio buffers.
- Fixed and ramped tempo maps resolve the same musical curve to the expected absolute frames.
- A target change demonstrably affects only its intended destination and role/scope.
- Smoothing/hysteresis remain finite and bounded; probability one always passes and probability zero always gates.
- M13 JSON remains readable and its compatibility adapter is unchanged.
- Planner controls, full destination coverage, matched-level critical listening and M14 target-machine sign-off are completed before M18 is marked complete.

## Listening protocol

Render the same seed and source material with no score sources, one slow filter or spatial macro, one event/role-relative phrase envelope, and one coupled modulation relationship. Compare full mix and isolated roles at matched perceived level. Listen for intelligible motion, stable transient identity, balanced brightness, controlled pitch behaviour and negative space around the foreground. Numeric tests establish timing and repeatability; they do not establish that the composition sounds better.

## Deliverables

- Typed target and lane-scope contracts with backward-compatible Score JSON.
- Deterministic source and modulation model on the shared beat/frame timeline.
- Production ScoreRenderer integration and focused regression tests.
- Minimal Score Control editor with source preview and offline re-render.
- Note-level envelope/vibrato consumers across the production melodic palettes.
- Remaining planner/synthesis/FX consumers, cross-platform CI evidence and a listening fixture report.
